What this is

Most material on AI agents is written for engineers. Fair enough, they build them. But engineers rarely decide which process gets an agent, how much it's allowed to do alone, who signs off when it gets something wrong, or whether the thing paid for itself. Managers do. So this course is written for them.

You'll still see real code. Every code path comes with a plain-English reading and a no-code alternative, because a manager who can read a forty-line agent loop asks much better questions in the steering meeting. And honestly, some of you will get hooked and build the whole thing yourselves. That's fine too.

There's no single "right" way to build an agent. A finance team living in Microsoft 365 should probably start in Copilot Studio. A startup with two Python developers might go straight to the raw API. A customer-service head buying a platform needs to understand Agentforce or ServiceNow well enough to negotiate. Pick the page that matches your situation, or let the method chooser suggest one.

The running example

Every build in this course uses the same small business problem, so you can compare methods like for like. Meridian Supplies is a fictional Pune distributor of office and facility supplies. Its salespeople constantly ask "can we give this customer X% off?" and the answer depends on the customer's tier, their payment record, the margin left after the discount, and who has authority to approve what.

It's a perfect first agent. Small enough to build in an afternoon. Real enough to have a policy, a judgement call and a human sign-off. And it fails in instructive ways if you get the design wrong.

The discount policy

Maximum discount: Bronze 5%, Silver 8%, Gold 12%. Customers with two or more late payments this financial year are capped at 3%. Gross margin after discount must stay at or above 12%. Anything above the cap needs Sales Manager approval; anything above 15% needs the Regional Head.

Try it yourself in the Agent Lab: pick a customer, ask for a discount, and step through what the agent does.

Ways to use the site

Self-study

Do one module a week, plus one method page that fits your track. Mark pages complete as you go; the bar in the left rail keeps count (it's stored in your own browser, nowhere else). Every module ends with a quiz and exercises with model answers.

In a classroom

The roadmap has a fourteen-week semester plan that maps sessions to modules, lab work and capstone milestones. The capstone page has team briefs, an assessment rubric and a demo-day guide. The media library collects every video and infographic in one place for slides.

At work

Skip to Level 3. Module 13's business-case template and Module 11's guardrail checklist are written to be lifted straight into a proposal. The enterprise platforms page is the one to read before a vendor demo.

A note on currency

This field moves monthly. Product names change, features ship, some tools get retired. Each page says "as of 2026" where it matters and links the official docs. When the site and the vendor disagree, believe the vendor.

What you need

For the no-code track: a browser and a free or work account on one of the assistant tools. For low-code: access to Copilot Studio, n8n (self-host or cloud trial) or Zapier. For code: Python 3.11 or newer, a terminal, and an API key from at least one model provider. Keep keys in environment variables, never in code, and set a monthly spend limit on the provider's console before you start. Use a small, cheap model for practice runs and save the big one for the final demo; Module 02 shows how to estimate the bill before you spend anything.

The Agent Lab and the chatbot demo run entirely in your browser. No key needed.
