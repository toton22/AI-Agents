Chatbots: from FAQ bot to agentic assistant

See three generations of chatbot answer the same employee questions, learn how to design and measure a bot people actually use, and build one from your own FAQ in under a minute.

35 min readTracked in your progress

Chatbot or agent?

Every agent has a chat window these days, so the words blur. Here's a working distinction. A chatbot holds a conversation and answers. An agent pursues a goal and acts: it looks things up in live systems, changes records, and decides what to do next. Most useful company bots in 2026 sit somewhere in between. They answer from policy documents most of the time and take a small number of carefully confirmed actions.

The history matters because you'll meet all of these generations inside the same company. The IT helpdesk might still run a menu bot from 2019 while marketing pilots an LLM assistant. Knowing which is which helps you judge what an upgrade would actually buy.

Manager's lens

Don't ask "should we use AI for the chatbot?" Ask "which questions need a live lookup or an action, and which just need the right paragraph of policy?" The answer tells you which generation you need, and the cheapest one that covers your top questions is usually right.

What to notice

  1. The brittleness of rules

    In rule-based mode, ask "how much time off am I entitled to?" Then ask "How many leave days do I get?"

    What happens

    The first fails because none of the keywords ("leave", "holiday", "vacation") appear. Same question, different words. Every rule-based bot in production has a long tail of these misses, which is why their fallback rate is the number to watch.

  2. Grounding and honesty

    In retrieval mode, ask "Can I bring my dog to office?" Then ask about travel allowance.

    What happens

    The dog question scores below the confidence threshold, so the bot says it doesn't know instead of inventing a pet policy. The allowance question retrieves the travel policy and cites it. "I don't know" is a feature you have to design in.

  3. Slots and confirmation

    In agentic mode, say "I need to apply for 2 days leave". Answer the questions it asks. Then try E-204 asking for 3 days.

    What happens

    The bot notices the missing employee ID (a "slot") and asks for it, checks the balance with a tool, and asks you to confirm before it submits anything. E-204 has zero earned leave, so the bot refuses to submit and explains why. That's the difference between answering and acting.

Build your own in a minute

Paste any FAQ in the box as Q: and A: lines, press Load into bot, and the retrieval bot above will answer from it instead. The example is an admissions FAQ for a fictional university; swap in your department's real questions. Nothing leaves your browser.

Tip

Write questions the way users actually ask them, not the way the policy is titled. "Can I pay in parts?" retrieves better than "Fee instalment facility". Better still, add two or three phrasings per answer. This is the same lesson as chunking in Module 05.

Designing a bot people use

Most failed chatbots fail on design, not technology. The model was fine; nobody decided what the bot was for, so it tried to do everything and did nothing well. Here's the checklist we use.

  • One job, stated up front. "I answer HR and IT policy questions and can check your leave balance." Users forgive limits they were told about.
  • The top 20 questions first. Pull six months of emails or tickets, count them, and make sure the bot nails the top 20 before anything else. They're usually most of the volume.
  • A real fallback. When unsure, say so and route: a form, an email address, or a live handover with the conversation attached so the human doesn't start from zero.
  • Confirm before acting. Any write action (submit leave, raise a ticket, change an address) gets a plain-language summary and a yes/no.
  • Cite the source. "Leave Policy §3" next to an answer builds trust and makes errors easy to spot.
  • Speak the users' language. In India that often means Hindi or a regional language, or Hinglish in a chat window. Test with real users, not just the project team.
  • Say it's a bot. Users should know they're talking to software, and how to reach a person.
  • Keep answers short. Two to four sentences, then offer more. Nobody reads a policy essay on a phone.

A complete helpdesk chatbot in about thirty lines. It puts a small FAQ file straight into the system prompt, which works well up to a few dozen pages. For hundreds of documents, switch to retrieval as shown in Module 05.

# pip install anthropic streamlit      then:  streamlit run helpdesk.py
import os
from pathlib import Path

import anthropic
import streamlit as st

client = anthropic.Anthropic()                      # reads ANTHROPIC_API_KEY from the environment
MODEL = os.getenv("MODEL", "claude-haiku-4-5-20251001")
FAQ = Path("helpdesk_faq.md").read_text(encoding="utf-8")

SYSTEM = f"""You are Meridian Supplies' HR and IT helpdesk assistant.
Answer ONLY from the policies below and name the section you used in brackets.
If the answer is not in the policies, say you don't know and suggest hr@meridian.example.
Keep answers under 80 words.
<policies>
{FAQ}
</policies>"""

st.title("Meridian Helpdesk")
if "messages" not in st.session_state:
    st.session_state.messages = []

for m in st.session_state.messages:                 # replay the conversation so far
    st.chat_message(m["role"]).write(m["content"])

if prompt := st.chat_input("Ask about leave, payroll, travel or IT"):
    st.session_state.messages.append({"role": "user", "content": prompt})
    st.chat_message("user").write(prompt)
    reply = client.messages.create(
        model=MODEL, max_tokens=500, system=SYSTEM,
        messages=st.session_state.messages,
    )
    text = reply.content[0].text
    st.session_state.messages.append({"role": "assistant", "content": text})
    st.chat_message("assistant").write(text)

What this does. Streamlit draws the chat window. Each time the user types, the whole conversation plus the policy text goes to the model with instructions to answer only from the policies and cite them. The model's reply is shown and saved so the next turn has context. There are no tools here, so it can't act; for that, add tool calling as in Module 04.

In India, a customer-facing bot often means WhatsApp. The route is the WhatsApp Business Platform (Meta's Cloud API), usually through a Business Solution Provider that handles the number, templates and billing. The bot logic itself can be any of the methods on this site; the provider passes messages in and out by webhook.

  1. Get a verified business number

    Through Meta Business Manager or a provider. Display name and business verification take days, so start early.

  2. Design message templates

    Messages you send first (reminders, confirmations) must use pre-approved templates. Replies within the customer-service window can be free text.

  3. Connect your bot by webhook

    Incoming messages arrive at your endpoint (an n8n workflow, a Copilot Studio channel, or your own API). Your bot replies through the provider's send API.

  4. Plan the human handover

    Route to a shared inbox where agents see the full chat. Tell the customer when a person takes over.

  5. Mind consent and data

    Opt-in for marketing messages, a clear privacy notice, and DPDP Act obligations for any personal data you store. Check Meta's current pricing model, which is per message category.

Measuring a chatbot

Vanity metric: number of conversations. Real metrics: did people get what they needed, and did it save anyone time? Track these from week one.

MetricWhat it meansHow to read it
Containment rateShare of conversations resolved without a humanHigh is good only if satisfaction is also high; a bot that traps people "contains" them too
Fallback rateShare of messages the bot couldn't handleRead the actual fallback messages weekly; they're your backlog
Escalation qualityDid handovers reach the right team with context?Ask the human agents; they know
CSAT / thumbs upUser rating after the chatLow response rates skew it; pair with containment
Answer accuracyShare of sampled answers that are correct and citedSample 50 a month and grade them; see Module 10
Time savedContained conversations × average human handling timeThe number your business case promised

In the business

Case · HR shared services

The leave-balance question

A manufacturing company's HR team found that around a third of employee queries were about leave balances and payslips. A retrieval bot alone couldn't help, because the answer lives in the HRMS, not a policy. Adding one read-only tool for balances moved that whole category to self-service. The lesson: look at where the answer lives before choosing the bot generation.

Case · Customer service

Order status on WhatsApp

A D2C brand launched a WhatsApp bot for "where is my order?" with a tracking-API lookup and a handover to agents for refunds. It kept refunds human on purpose: they involve money and judgement, and complaints there carry the most reputational risk.

Case · Admissions

The 11 pm applicant

University admissions teams get most questions in the evening, when counsellors are offline. An FAQ bot on the website and WhatsApp that answers eligibility, fees and deadlines, and books a counsellor callback for anything else, catches leads that would otherwise go to a competitor's site. See Capstone 11.

Pitfalls

  • Launching without the top 20. If the most common question fails, users leave and don't come back.
  • No owner. Policies change; someone must update the knowledge base and read the fallbacks every week.
  • Trapping users. No visible way to reach a human is the fastest route to angry social-media posts.
  • Answering from stale documents. Last year's travel policy quoted confidently is worse than no answer.
  • Letting it act without confirmation. One wrong cancellation or refund costs more than the bot saved that month.
  • Pasting personal data into unapproved tools while prototyping. Use test data until IT and legal have signed off.

Quiz

Employees mostly ask about their own leave balance and payslip dates. Which bot generation do you need at minimum?

  1. Rule-based menu bot
  2. Retrieval bot over the HR policy PDF
  3. A bot with a read-only tool connected to the HR system
  4. A multi-agent system

Balances live in the HR system, not in a document, so retrieval can't answer them. One read-only tool is enough; multi-agent is overkill.

Your bot's containment rate is 85% but CSAT is falling. What's the most likely problem?

  1. The model is too small
  2. Users can't easily reach a human, so they give up rather than get help
  3. Containment is too low
  4. The bot is answering too fast

High containment with falling satisfaction usually means people are stuck, not served. Check the handover path and the conversations that ended without resolution.

Why did the retrieval bot in the demo say "I don't know" about bringing a dog to the office?

  1. No policy passage scored above its confidence threshold
  2. Dogs are banned in the policy
  3. It ran out of tokens
  4. The rule list had no "dog" keyword

The retriever found nothing similar enough, and the bot was designed to admit that instead of guessing. (The keyword answer describes the rule-based bot.)

Which action should a helpdesk bot confirm with the user before doing it?

  1. Looking up the leave policy
  2. Checking a leave balance
  3. Showing a payslip date
  4. Submitting a leave request

Reads are safe to do immediately. Writes change records and notify people, so they get a summary and a yes/no.

A WhatsApp bot wants to send a payment reminder to a customer who hasn't messaged in weeks. What's required?

  1. Nothing special; WhatsApp is free-form
  2. A pre-approved message template and the customer's opt-in
  3. A voice call first
  4. A Custom GPT

Business-initiated messages on the WhatsApp Business Platform use approved templates, and you need the customer's consent to message them.

Key takeaways

  • Pick the bot generation by where the answers live: documents need retrieval, records need tools, actions need confirmation.
  • Design beats technology: one job, the top 20 questions, an honest fallback and a visible route to a human.
  • Measure containment together with satisfaction and accuracy, never alone.
  • In India, plan for WhatsApp and for more than one language from the start.
